# The plif name

The code is Apache-2.0. The name is not part of the code.

Section 6 of the Apache License grants no rights to trade names, trademarks, or
product names. This file says plainly what that means here, so nobody has to
guess and nobody can claim they did not know.

## What you may do without asking

- Use plif at work, at home, inside a company, inside a paid product.
- Fork it, change anything, and sell what you build.
- Say your product **"is built on plif"**, **"uses plif"**, or **"is a fork of
  plif"**. Factual statements about where your code came from are fine and are
  encouraged.
- Write about plif, review it, teach it, package it for a distribution.

## What requires a different name

If you distribute a modified version, give it your own name.

- Do not call your fork "plif", "plif CLI", or anything a reasonable person
  would read as the official project.
- Do not use "plif" as the name of your product, your company, or your service.
- Do not imply endorsement by, or partnership with, this project or its author.
- Do not use the author's name — Anthophicous — to promote your version.

Renaming a fork is a small amount of work. Being told your users installed the
wrong thing, or that a bug you never wrote is attributed to you, is not.

## Attribution

Apache-2.0 §4 already requires you to carry the `NOTICE` file into anything you
redistribute. Keep it where the people receiving your copy can actually read
it: in the source tree, in the documentation, or in an about screen. A NOTICE
buried in a build artifact nobody opens satisfies the letter and misses the
point.

## If you are unsure

Open an issue and ask. The answer is usually yes.
