# Experience archetypes — product pressure, not templates

Read this only for a new/full surface or when the product's dominant behavior is materially unclear. An archetype predicts hierarchy, density, interaction and expressive budget; it does not prescribe a visual style.

Choose one dominant archetype and at most one supporting archetype. Record them in `product_frame.experience_archetype` with evidence and `impact_if_false`. A surface that seems to need three or more usually contains multiple flows that should be framed separately.

## Archetype matrix

| Archetype | User is trying to | Design pressure | Identity can live in | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CONVINCE | understand value and decide | narrative order, credible evidence, objection handling, clear conversion | art-directed composition, type voice, proof presentation, one memorable demonstration | generic centered hero, invented metrics/testimonials, feature-card wallpaper |
| OPERATE | perform repeated work | stable placement, low latency, compact rhythm, keyboard continuity, recoverable state | control rhythm, information architecture, domain-specific status language | marketing whitespace, theatrical delay, unstable navigation |
| ANALYZE | compare, diagnose or forecast | alignment, filters, provenance, uncertainty, dense scanning | data typography, meaningful color, comparison model, annotation | dashboard cosplay, decorative charts, every number in a card |
| CREATE | manipulate material and iterate | direct manipulation, canvas/workspace focus, commands, history, reversibility | tool behavior, spatial model, artifact presentation, completion feedback | chrome competing with the work, hidden destructive state, gratuitous modal chains |
| COMPLETE | finish a bounded flow | sequence, confidence, validation, recovery, mobile keyboard, progress truth | pacing, reassurance, content voice, transition between milestones | punitive validation, decorative detours, fake completion |
| EXPLORE | discover a catalog, story, place or body of work | orientation, curiosity, filtering, spatial/narrative continuity | imagery, editorial rhythm, transition motif, controlled surprise | disorientation as creativity, media without labels or fallback |
| LEARN | build understanding or retrieve knowledge | reading measure, hierarchy, examples, wayfinding, progressive disclosure | typographic system, diagrams, annotations, content choreography | tiny gray copy, over-animation, ornamental navigation |
| MONITOR | notice change and respond | state salience, chronology, confidence, latency, escalation paths | alert grammar, temporal visualization, operational density | low-contrast status, noisy motion, equal emphasis everywhere |
| BELONG | participate and recognize people/culture | identity, contribution, moderation, social context, continuity | authentic member content, rituals, expressive but legible voice | fabricated community proof, generic avatars, engagement-dark-patterns |

## Product-to-art-direction bridge

Before visual options, write a compact brief whose fields can be falsified:

```text
EXPERIENCE ARCHETYPE  dominant + supporting, with evidence
CONTENT TRUTH         real nouns, evidence, assets and content volume available
EMOTIONAL TRAJECTORY  how the user should feel at entry -> decision/action -> settlement
VISUAL THESIS         one sentence connecting product truth to formal behavior
COMPOSITION LAW       grid, asymmetry, overlap, density, negative-space and focal logic
TYPE VOICE            role of display/body/data/mono; why this voice fits
COLOR / MATERIAL / LIGHT
                      dominance, contrast, surface physics and illumination logic
ASSET WORLD           photography/illustration/product media/diagram/3D/none; crop and treatment
MOTION PREMISE        what moves, why, tempo and spatial model
SIGNATURE RELATIONSHIP
                      one product-specific memorable mechanism
COUNTER-DEFAULT       nearest plausible generic treatment explicitly refused
ANTI-GOALS            visual or behavioral moves that would betray this product
```

Transfer the visual fields into DesignDNA. Do not use mood adjectives alone: “premium”, “clean”, “modern” and “playful” are incomplete until they alter composition, typography, material, motion or content treatment.

## Decision tests

A direction is not ready when any answer is weak:

1. Could the same page sell an unrelated AI/SaaS product after only logo, accent and copy changes? If yes, the thesis is generic.
2. Does the hierarchy still express the archetype in grayscale, with effects disabled and the logo removed?
3. Is the signature tied to a product job or truth rather than a library demo?
4. Does realistic content volume strengthen the design rather than break it?
5. Can narrow, reduced-motion, reduced-transparency and low-power variants preserve the same identity?
