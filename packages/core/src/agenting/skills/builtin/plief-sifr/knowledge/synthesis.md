# Design concept synthesis

Use concepts as historical and operational lenses. They are not presets and do
not override product truth, content requirements, accessibility or the host
design system.

## Required synthesis record

```text
dominant_concept: <id>
supporting_concepts: [<id>]
inherited_rules: [rules that survive from a source]
rejected_rules: [rules intentionally not imported]
tensions: [specific incompatibilities and resolution]
product_cause: <what user/job made this direction useful>
signature_relationship: <one memorable spatial/type/content relationship>
counter_default: <one deliberate refusal of a generic pattern>
confidence: VERIFIED | INFERRED | HYPOTHESIS | UNVERIFIED
```

A dominant concept supplies grammar, not decoration. A supporting concept may
contribute one bounded behavior or material law. If a combination cannot name
ownership, purpose and a responsive/accessibility consequence, reject it.

## Examples of honest tension

- `swiss-typographic` + `maximalism`: use the former for alignment and the latter
  for controlled scale/contrast; do not stack random color and type.
- `japanese-minimalism` + `data-dense`: preserve negative-space rhythm around
  decision-critical clusters; do not shrink content into pale microtext.
- `liquid-glass` + `editorial`: reserve glass for a functional top layer while
  keeping reading surfaces opaque and stable.
- `spatial-immersive` + `accessibility`: keep the spatial explanation optional;
  expose the same facts and controls in semantic DOM.
